## THEME: math-primitives  (engine/math (matt_math.*, ericson_math.*, collision_tools.*) - vectors, predicates, barycentric, convex hulls, Voronoi regions, Minkowski)

The honest state of engine/math against Chapter 3 is that the book is already in the building, and the parts of it that were ported are correct. `ericson_math.cpp` carries fifteen live ports with the source credited at the top, and the ones that matter are right: `closest_pt_point_triangle` is the full seven-region Voronoi form, `closest_pt_point_OBB` inverts the box frame with two dot products rather than a determinant (which is the concrete reason deleting MatrixF cost nothing), `test_2D_segment_segment` keeps the a4 = a3 + a2 - a1 identity with the naive fourth call commented out beside it, `signed_2D_tri_area` is exactly ORIENT2D in the numerically preferable translated form, and `narrow_phase`'s `test_axis` deliberately derives the normal's direction from the projection intervals rather than from vertex winding, which is a better choice than the book's own ComputePlane convention and would already have saved it on triangles. The 3D material was translated rather than restated: the OBB loop runs `i < 2` with a comment saying why, the moving-AABB test is x/y slabs only, and `test_triangle_AABB` was left commented out rather than half-ported. Mining stopped at page 132, and the file says so.

What Chapter 3 exposes is not a missing algorithm but a missing name. One scalar — the 2D cross, the perp-dot, ORIENT2D, twice the signed triangle area — is the primitive from which sidedness, winding, convexity, collinearity, point-in-triangle and polygon area all fall out, and the engine has written it three times: once correctly but under a triangle-shaped name that nothing else reaches for, and twice broken. `Vector2F::cross` returns the two products of the determinant without subtracting them; `mattmath::sign` computes the right expression and truncates it to int, so every sliver under one square unit of area reports collinear. Both are dead, which makes them cheap to remove and dangerous to leave, because each is the method the next reader will reach for by name. Give that scalar one home and four separate defects downstream become one-line fixes.

The through-line that would most improve this area runs through `Quad`. `Quad::is_valid()` allocates a `std::vector<Segment>` and runs six edge-pair tests on every construction and every vertex write, and it enforces the wrong invariant — it rejects bowties and accepts darts, while both of its consumers, SAT in `narrow_phase` and the fixed-diagonal fan in `Quad::triangles()`, are only sound on convex shapes. The book's IsConvexQuad is one interior-only diagonal test, six times cheaper, allocation-free, and strictly stronger. It is also urgent for a reason neither the book nor the mining agents alone would surface: the review backlog already carries a remedy that closes `segments_intersect`'s open boundary with the contract "touching intersects", and four of `is_valid()`'s six pairs are adjacent edges sharing an endpoint that currently pass only because the test is strict. The day that remedy lands, every `Quad` constructor in the tree throws. Fixing the predicate first removes the coupling entirely. The same shape of problem sits under `Triangle`, which has no validity check at all, and a collinear triangle passes both of `narrow_phase`'s degeneracy guards to produce a manifold with a real normal and a positive penetration for a shape with no interior.

Two things need saying about scale, because the raw candidates consistently overstate it. First, `engine/collision` is not on any hot path today: `narrow_phase` and `find_contacts` are referenced only by their own module and by tests, and `game/` touches nothing in `engine/collision` except `partitioner.h`. Every performance argument here — including the strongest one, that a rectangle's four edge normals are the cardinal axes and rect-vs-rect therefore pays eight square roots and eight axis tests where two suffice — is an argument about what that path will cost after C2 ports the game onto it, which makes now the right time to fix it and the wrong time to claim a regression. Second, `Quad` and `RectangleRotated` are never constructed outside `engine/math` and tests, so their per-mutation validation costs are engine-API traps rather than frame time. The one place the book lands directly on live game code is the ramp: `Player::on_structure_ramp_collision` answers a slope contact with `set_velocity_y(0.0f)` in five branches, deleting a player's climb rate instead of removing the into-surface component, and section 3.3.3's parallel/perpendicular decomposition is the two-multiply `slide()` that belongs next to `separation()` and `separation_along()` in `resolve.h` — the piece that lets C2 delete the eight cardinal handlers rather than wrap them.

### Quad::is_valid() must test convexity — and fixing it is a prerequisite for a change already in the review backlog  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 3.7.1, pp.59-62 (IsConvexQuad; the transverse-diagonal criterion; Figure 3.18's adversarial corpus). Reinforced by 3.4 p.52 (convex combination) and 3.8 p.64 (SAT is a theorem about convex sets).
what: For a quadrilateral ABCD, convexity is exactly equivalent to the two diagonals AC and BD crossing on their interiors. The book implements it as two sign comparisons; in 2D each Cross collapses to the scalar perp-dot, so it is either one interior-only segment test or four same-sign signed areas around the boundary. Non-crossing diagonals mean concave (dart), self-intersecting (bowtie), or degenerate. Four multiply-subtract pairs, no branches, no allocation, and it establishes the winding as a side effect.
where: engine/math/matt_math.cpp:2886-2903 (Quad::is_valid), called from all five constructors (2754, 2769, 2787, 2800, 2814) and all four setters (2936, 2946, 2956, 2966). Consumers: engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:59-68 (quad branch) and :69-79 (rectangle_rotated branch, which builds a Quad first), engine/math/matt_math.cpp:3018 (Quad::triangles), :509 (quad_point_intersect), :590 (point_rectangle_rotated_intersect). Test corpus goes in tests/math/math_tests.cpp, which builds every single Quad from an axis-aligned RectangleF (lines 341-361, 524-540, 636-652, 722-738, 797-814).
why: Two separate things are wrong and one is scheduled to detonate. First, is_valid() builds a std::vector<Segment> via edges() and runs six pairwise segment tests, which rejects bowties and accepts darts — but SAT (narrow_phase) and the fixed-diagonal fan (Quad::triangles) are both only decision procedures for convex shapes, so a dart yields a silently wrong manifold and silently wrong containment. Second, four of those six pairs are adjacent edges sharing an endpoint by construction, and they report 'no intersection' only because test_2D_segment_segment uses strict a1*a2 < 0.0f, which makes a shared endpoint a zero product. docs/review/round-2/all-findings.md:5261-5326 already carries a remedy that closes that boundary with the contract 'collinear overlap intersects; touching intersects'. The moment that lands, every Quad constructor in the tree throws. The convexity rewrite removes the coupling entirely, is strictly stronger, is one test instead of six, and deletes a heap allocation from every Quad construction and mutation.
philosophy: Serves T2 (the constructor enforces a weaker invariant than its only consumers require), T11 and T8 (a std::vector allocation inside a value type's constructor and every setter), T4 ('the diagonals cross' is a shorter sentence than 'no two of six edge pairs meet'). Fights nothing. The contract obligation the project already owns applies: the header must state what a degenerate quad (coincident or collinear vertices) answers, and Figure 3.18 supplies the exact test inputs.
corrections: The mining agents were right on every mechanical detail here, including line numbers. Two refinements. (1) The claim that this is a live per-frame cost is wrong: Quad and RectangleRotated are never constructed in game/ or samples/ — grep finds no use of either type outside engine/math, engine/collision and tests. So narrow_phase.cpp:71's per-pair Quad construction for rotated rectangles is a real code path with zero live traffic. This is an engine-API correctness fix, not a frame-time fix. (2) The one-diagonal form needs the same degenerate answer written down that the four-area form does: segments_intersect short-circuits on `a == b` (matt_math.cpp:542-548) before reaching the strict test, which is unreachable for a quad's two diagonals but is the kind of thing that makes a rewrite behave differently than expected.

### Give MattMath one correct 2D cross product; delete the two broken encodings of it  [adapt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 3.1.6.1, pp.32-33 (ORIENT2D as the translated 2x2 determinant) and 3.3.5, pp.41-43 (there is no 3D cross product in 2D; the analogue is the scalar perp-dot). Also 3.1.3, pp.27-28 (a 2x2 determinant IS a signed parallelogram area).
what: One scalar answers three questions: perp_dot(u,v) = u.x*v.y - u.y*v.x. Its sign is the winding of the pair (positive when v is counter-clockwise from u), its magnitude is the signed parallelogram area, and it is zero exactly when the two are parallel. Applied to points, ORIENT2D(a,b,c) = perp_dot(a-c, b-c) is twice the signed area of triangle abc, and its sign is 'which side of directed line ab does c lie on'. Sidedness, winding, area and collinearity are one arithmetic object, not four.
where: engine/math/ericson_math.cpp:358-362 (signed_2D_tri_area — the correct one, declared ericson_math.h:117); engine/math/matt_math.cpp:1359-1362 (Vector2F::cross); engine/math/matt_math.cpp:65-70 and matt_math.h:57 (mattmath::sign). Downstream: ericson_math.cpp:372-382 (test_2D_segment_segment), and every finding below that needs a sidedness test.
why: The engine has three encodings of one primitive and two of them are broken. Vector2F::cross returns Vector2F(x*other.y, y*other.x) — the two products of the determinant, never subtracted. It is not the cross product, not the perp-dot, not the component-wise product, and not any quantity with a geometric meaning. mattmath::sign computes the correct expression and then wraps it in static_cast<int>, so any triple whose doubled signed area lies in (-1, 1) reports 0, i.e. collinear — which is precisely the input a sidedness predicate exists to arbitrate; the name also lies, because it returns a truncated area rather than a sign. Both have zero callers anywhere in the repository, so this costs two deletions. The surviving correct implementation is buried under a triangle-shaped name whose header comment says only 'Returns 2 times the signed triangle area', so nothing else reaches for it. Add `static float Vector2F::cross(const Vector2F&, const Vector2F&)` beside dot(), express signed_2D_tri_area as cross(a-c, b-c), and say in ericson_math.h that this is ORIENT2D and that its sign is a sidedness test. Four of the findings below become available the moment it has a name.
philosophy: T9 above all — hand-rolling the math is deliberate, which makes getting it right the engine's job rather than a shrug. T2 (a public method named cross() that returns garbage is a loaded gun for exactly the kind of book-following work this exercise produces). T4 (one named operation beats three unnamed near-misses). No tension.
corrections: All three code claims verified exactly, at the line numbers given, and the zero-caller claims hold repo-wide including samples/ and tests/. One refinement the agents did not make: keeping the name `cross` rather than introducing `perp_dot` is the better call for T12 and for a reader coming from the book, because the book itself calls the 2D scalar the pseudo cross product and derives it from the 3D cross — but the header must say it returns a scalar and why, or the next reader assumes the old broken signature.

### Rect-vs-rect is every real contact, and narrow_phase pays eight square roots to rediscover four constants  [adapt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 3.11, pp.70-72 (the Minkowski difference of two AABBs is an AABB: (a.min - b.max, a.max - b.min); A and B intersect iff it contains the origin). With 3.6, p.55 (a precomputed plane normal should already be unit — establish it once, not per query) and 3.4, p.50 (a convex region as an intersection of slabs; parallel edges share one slab).
what: A 2D AABB's four edge normals are (0,1), (-1,0), (0,-1), (1,0): already unit, known without arithmetic, and only two distinct up to sign. Because SAT's per-axis test is sign-symmetric, testing an axis and its negation is pure duplication. So two AABBs need exactly two axes between them, not eight — and the answer is the Minkowski box directly: penetration is the smallest of {a.right - b.left, b.right - a.left, a.bottom - b.top, b.bottom - a.top}, with the normal read off from which one won. No square root, no dot product, roughly eight subtractions.
where: engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:179-209 — the edge loop calls Vector2F(-edge.y, edge.x).normalized() per edge (one std::sqrt and two divides) and then test_axis, which projects four points per polygon. engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:38-49 (polygon_from's rectangle branch, which emits TL, TR, BR, BL from bounding_box()). The special case is a `case` at the top of narrow_phase() in the same file; the equivalence test goes in tests/collision/narrow_phase_tests.cpp beside the existing 41x25 sweep at line 157.
why: I traced the four edge normals polygon_from actually produces for a rectangle and they are exactly the four cardinal axes, in that order. I also traced test_axis (narrow_phase.cpp:116-160) under axis negation: min and max swap and negate, so `forwards` and `backwards` exchange, the separated condition is identical, the penetration is identical, and the chosen normal is identical. The negated axis is therefore provably redundant. That is 8 sqrt and 8 axis tests (each 8 dot products) doing the work of 2 axis tests and no sqrt, on the one pair type that is every collision the game runs — players, all five projectile types, paint tiles, structures — at roughly 600-1000 live projectiles. The book's Minkowski framing gives the closed form outright, which is cheaper still than the two-axis SAT.
philosophy: T8 is a headline value and this is the single hottest engine function once the game is ported onto find_contacts. The honest cost is T4: a second code path that must agree with the first, which is why the randomised equivalence test against the general path is part of the finding rather than an optional extra. It does not fight T3 — the two paths compute the same answer, not a cheaper approximation.
corrections: The agents present this as a live per-frame cost. It is not, yet: grep shows narrow_phase is referenced only by engine/collision/contacts.cpp, its own header, and tests/collision/narrow_phase_tests.cpp — game/ touches nothing in engine/collision except partitioner.h (level.h:19, level_builder.h:15). The ~10^6 pair tests per frame are currently spent in six copies of a hand-written is_colliding block in game/objects/, not here. So this is a change to make *before* C2 ports the game onto find_contacts, on the strength of what that path will cost, not a regression to fix today. I also reject the related slice-3 proposal to defer normalisation generally by comparing penetration^2 cross-multiplied by squared axis lengths: it is less readable, changes tie-breaking at the margin, and PLAN records that no benchmark harness exists — the rectangle special case gets the same win with none of that risk.

### slide(): remove the into-surface component of a velocity instead of deleting the whole axis  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 3.3.3, pp.40-41 (unique decomposition of v against a direction u into parallel p = ((u.v)/(u.u))u and perpendicular q = v - p; Figure 3.5b). For unit u, p = (u.v)u.
what: Any vector decomposes uniquely against a direction into a component along it and a component perpendicular to it. With a unit contact normal in hand, the tangential part of a velocity is v - normal * dot(v, normal). No trig, no square root, no branch chain. In 2D the perpendicular part is unambiguous because there is only one perpendicular direction.
where: Add to engine/collision/resolve.h beside separation() and separation_along(), which already own exactly this vocabulary (resolve.h:20-33 documents separation_along in the book's own projection terms). The caller it replaces is game/objects/player.cpp:280-360 — on_structure_ramp_collision, which answers every ramp contact with CollisionTools::resolve_object_collision(..., DIRECTION_DOWN) followed by set_velocity_y(0.0f), in five separate branches.
why: I read the ramp path. It sets velocity_y to zero outright, which deletes the whole vertical velocity rather than only the component into the surface, so a player running up a slope loses their climb rate and a player landing on one stops dead instead of sliding. The correct response, given a manifold, is one subtraction. This is also the piece that lets C2 *delete* Player::on_structure_ramp_collision and on_structure_collision's eight cardinal handlers rather than merely wrap them: narrow_phase already returns the ramp's slope normal analytically and there is a pinning test for it (tests/collision/narrow_phase_tests.cpp:123, 'a ramp resolves along its slope, not along an axis'). Contract note the engine's house style requires: return the velocity unchanged when dot(v, normal) is non-positive, because a body already moving away from a surface is not being stopped by it.
philosophy: T3 exactly — the simplest believable model of a slope is 'you keep your tangential speed', and that is also the physical one, so simplicity costs nothing here. T2, because zeroing velocity_y is wrong rather than approximate. T8: two multiplies and a subtract. It continues the committed shape of resolve.h — the engine offers the arithmetic and never performs it, because who moves is a gameplay question.
corrections: The agents cite 'Player::on_collision (player.cpp:280-317)'. The actual function is Player::on_structure_ramp_collision at player.cpp:254-380, reached from Player::on_collision at :245-247, and set_velocity_y(0.0f) appears in five branches, not one. The substance is right. One thing they missed that strengthens it: the resolution itself is also wrong, not just the velocity — every branch resolves DIRECTION_DOWN via CollisionTools, which snaps the player against the ramp's bounding box, not its hypotenuse.

### Make test_point_triangle three sign comparisons — which makes barycentric() dead code  [adapt | value 4 | effort S]
book: 3.4, pp.49-52 (the areal form u = SignedArea(PBC)/SignedArea(ABC) etc.; Figure 3.10's seven regions, which are decided by the *signs* of u, v, w alone; TestPointTriangle p.52). The book's projection-plane-selection step vanishes in 2D.
what: Containment needs only the signs of the three sub-triangle areas relative to the sign of the whole, never their magnitudes, so the division by the total area drops out completely: p is inside abc iff signed_2D_tri_area(p,b,c), signed_2D_tri_area(a,p,c) and signed_2D_tri_area(a,b,p) all share the sign of signed_2D_tri_area(a,b,c), or are zero if the boundary is closed. Six multiplies, three subtracts, no division, winding-independent, and it cannot produce a NaN.
where: engine/math/ericson_math.cpp:416-422 (test_point_triangle) and :399-413 (barycentric). Reached from engine/math/matt_math.cpp:426-430 (triangle_point_intersect / Triangle::contains), which is called four times per player-vs-ramp test from rectangle_triangle_intersect (matt_math.cpp:171-207, lines 187-192), and from triangle_rectangle_rotated_intersect (:436-445) and quad_point_intersect (:509-521).
why: barycentric() divides by denom = d00*d11 - d01*d01 with no guard (ericson_math.cpp:409-411). For a collinear or coincident-vertex triangle that is zero, v and w come back inf or NaN, and test_point_triangle's `v >= 0.0f && w >= 0.0f` then returns false through NaN comparisons — safe by luck, not by design. The sign form needs no validity check at all: a degenerate triangle simply contains nothing, because all four areas are zero and the strict comparison fails. It is also cheaper (six multiplies against ten multiplies plus two divisions) and it reads as the geometry — 'same side of all three edges' — rather than as a linear-algebra indirection. rectangle_triangle_intersect is the one non-rect-rect pair the game actually runs, four times per player per frame against the single king_of_the_hill ramp.
philosophy: T2 (removes an unguarded division and a NaN path from the engine's only point-in-triangle primitive), T8 (no divide), T4 (three sign comparisons over a Cramer solve followed by an interval test). T3 loses nothing: the sign test is if anything the more numerically honest of the two.
corrections: Two slices assert that barycentric() must stay 'because closest_pt_point_triangle needs the weights, not just the sign'. That is false. closest_pt_point_triangle (ericson_math.cpp:46-90) computes its own va, vb, vc inline and never calls barycentric. I grepped the whole tree: barycentric()'s only caller is test_point_triangle, and test_point_triangle's only caller is triangle_point_intersect. So taking the sign form leaves barycentric() with zero callers, and the honest move is to delete it along with its unguarded division rather than keep it as a public primitive nothing uses. That also settles the competing slice-4 proposal to hoist d00/d01/d11 per triangle: reject it, there is nothing left to hoist.

### Triangle has no validity check, and a collinear triangle produces a phantom manifold  [adapt | value 4 | effort M]
book: 3.5, p.54 (collinearity test: three points are collinear iff the area of ABC is zero). The noncollinearity precondition is stated three separate times in the slice — 3.4 p.46 for the barycentric parameterisation, 3.5 p.54 standalone, 3.6 p.55 in ComputePlane's own comment.
what: Three points are collinear exactly when their signed area is zero; in 2D that is one signed_2D_tri_area call against a tolerance, no cross product and no square root. The book treats noncollinearity as a precondition on the data, checked where the data enters the system.
where: mattmath::Triangle (engine/math/matt_math.h:770-821) — no is_valid(), no throwing constructor, unlike Quad (matt_math.cpp:2754+) and RectangleRotated (matt_math.cpp:3525) which both throw. The consequence lives at engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:168 and :212. The relative-tolerance precedent to copy is RectangleRotated::edges_valid (matt_math.cpp:3625-3632), which uses EPSILON * std::max(1.0f, length).
why: narrow_phase guards only polygon.count < 3 (line 168) and the all-edges-degenerate case (line 212). A *collinear* triangle whose three edges are each individually nonzero passes both: its edge perpendiculars normalise to real unit axes, it projects to a zero-width interval on its own edge normals, and the function returns a Manifold with a nonzero normal and a positive penetration for a shape that has no interior. narrow_phase.h's own stated rule is that 'silently missing collisions is the worse failure' — silently inventing one is the same failure. A signed-area check is one multiply-subtract pair, and the tolerance must be relative because an area scales with the square of the input scale, which is the one thing none of the engine's five current tolerance sites agrees on.
philosophy: T2: the failure mode is a phantom contact, and the engine's own convention says a primitive states its preconditions and whether they are checked. T5 is unreachable here because triangle points are runtime data, so the check is a constructor throw matching Quad and RectangleRotated. Against T8: one signed area per Triangle construction, and Triangle is constructed inside Quad::triangles() on the containment paths — which is an argument for killing those triangle-splitting paths at the same time, not for skipping the check.
corrections: Verified: Triangle's four constructors (matt_math.h:774-782) do no validation at all, and the class has no is_valid(). One thing the agents did not weigh: adding a throwing constructor to Triangle is a wider blast radius than it looks, because TriangleRightAxisAligned derives from it and level_object_builder.cpp:147-153 constructs one straight from JSON floats. That is the right place for the throw to land — bad level data should fail loudly at load — but it means this item owes a decision about what happens to a malformed level file, which is why it is M and not S.

### Delete Vector2F::rotate_vector_by_ref — it reads its own half-written output  [adopt | value 4 | effort S]
book: 3.1.1, pp.25-26 (matrix multiplication: every entry cij of the result is formed from the ORIGINAL entries of A and B).
what: A 2D rotation is the matrix [[cos,-sin],[sin,cos]] applied to a column vector, so both output components must be computed from the original x and y. Writing the new x into the input before computing the new y evaluates the second row against a mixture of old and new state, which is not a rotation of anything.
where: engine/math/matt_math.cpp:1449-1456 (Vector2F::rotate_vector_by_ref), declared matt_math.h:468. The two correct siblings are Vector2F::rotate (matt_math.cpp:1406-1416, two temporaries) and the static Vector2F::rotate_vector (matt_math.cpp:1441-1448, correct by construction).
why: The body is literally `vec.x = vec.x * cos_angle - vec.y * sin_angle;` followed by `vec.y = vec.x * sin_angle + vec.y * cos_angle;` — the second line reads the x it just overwrote. It has zero callers anywhere in the repository. The tree therefore contains one correct pattern twice and one broken copy of it, all three public on the same value type for one concept. Delete it; rotate() already is the by-reference version. This is not a matrix-subsystem resurrection — MatrixF is deleted and should stay deleted — it is one broken 2x2 product on a value type.
philosophy: T2 and T4. Deleting rather than repairing serves T4 harder: Vector2F currently exposes rotate, rotate_vector and rotate_vector_by_ref for a single operation, and the one that is wrong is the one whose name is longest and therefore most likely to be picked by someone skimming the header.
corrections: Verified verbatim at the stated lines, and the zero-caller claim holds repo-wide.

### Write the contract the engine's own rules demand: what zero means, and that convexity is the precondition  [adopt | value 4 | effort S]
book: 3.1.6, p.32 (determinant predicates in floating point 'are very sensitive to rounding error' and algorithms relying on a correct sign in degenerate configurations 'are likely not to work as intended'). 3.8, p.64 (SAT holds for convex sets; 'the same is not necessarily true for concave sets').
what: Two contract sentences the book states and the engine does not. First: a determinant predicate is trustworthy when the answer is unambiguous and untrustworthy exactly at the boundary, so the library must pick which side zero belongs to and say so once. Second: the separating-axis theorem is a theorem about convex sets — failing to find a separating axis proves overlap only if both arguments are convex, and for concave sets finding none proves nothing.

### Point-in-convex-polygon by edge halfplanes, not by splitting into triangles  [adapt | value 3 | effort S]

what: A convex polygon is the intersection of its edge halfplanes, so a point is inside iff it is on the inside of every edge. where: engine/math/matt_math.cpp:509-521 (quad_point_intersect) and :590-596 (point_rectangle_rotated_intersect, which builds a Quad first). The allocation is engine/math/matt_math.cpp:3018-3026 (Quad::triangles), and the two solves are the barycentric path in ericson_math.cpp:399-421.

### Compare the signs of two determinants; do not multiply them  [adapt | value 3 | effort S]
book: 3.1.6, p.32 ('the sign of a determinant plays a special role... often used as topological predicates'), and the book's caution that direct evaluation of a predicate expression is not generally its most robust form.

where: engine/math/ericson_math.cpp:375 (`if (a1 * a2 < 0.0f)`) and :382 (`if (a3 * a4 < 0.0f)`), inside test_2D_segment_segment — reached from segments_intersect (matt_math.cpp:542-560), and thence from Quad::is_valid, triangles_intersect, triangle_segment_intersect and segment_rectangle_rotated_intersect.

## THEME: bounding-volumes  (bounding volumes - AABB, circle, OBB/RectangleRotated, k-DOP, computing and updating them, Shape::bounding_box and Shape::inflate)

The engine's bounding-volume story is in better shape than the raw candidates suggest in one respect and worse in another. Better: `mattmath::RectangleF` is exactly Ericson's min-width AABB struct, the representation his §4.2 says is right for a world where everything translates and nothing rotates — and everything in this game translates and nothing rotates. `RectangleF::bounding_box()` returns `*this`, so for the shape that is 99% of the content the bound IS the geometry. `Triangle`/`Quad`/`RectangleRotated::bounding_box()` are the textbook extremal-point sweep over 3-4 vertices, which the book itself says is optimal at that vertex count and explicitly says convex-hull preprocessing cannot improve. `RectangleF::union_of` is the O(1) grow, and `UiContainer::bounds()` (engine/ui/widget.cpp:96-121) already carries the hard-won degenerate-input lesson that `RectangleF::ZERO` is a point at the origin, not an empty set. The engine got the ladder rung right and got it right for the reason the book gives.

Worse: nothing in the engine treats a bounding volume as a *thing you compute once and reuse*. `Shape::bounding_box()` is a virtual call returning a 24-byte polymorphic value (8 of which are a vptr the broad phase will never dereference), and `Shape::AABB_intersects` (matt_math.cpp:122-125) makes two of them per pair. `find_contacts` calls that inside an all-pairs loop. `RectangleRotated` does cache — and caches into a `std::vector<Point2F>` rebuilt by heap allocation in every mutator, inside a value type. And `narrow_phase`'s `polygon_from` builds a `mattmath::Quad` from a `RectangleRotated` by a path that heap-allocates three times and runs `Quad::is_valid()` twice, inside the one function whose own header comment says it avoids `Shape::edges()` precisely to dodge that allocation. The book's §4.2.2 caching argument and §4.2.6 centre-radius identity between them delete every one of those.

The single sharpest finding is not a technique but a proof. Ericson states on p.102 that the "is either body wholly outside a face of the other" test is exact in 2D and fails only in 3D — the nine cross-product axes of Table 4.1 exist solely to catch a 3D edge-on-edge configuration that has no 2D counterpart. `narrow_phase.h` documents at length what it replaces and why the old answer was wrong; it never says its axis set is *complete*. It is, and the book says so. That converts "believed correct" into "provably exact", forecloses a future reader adding cross-product axes from published 3D SAT code, and closes the question of whether a GJK path is ever needed for accuracy. It costs three lines of comment.

The through-line, if there is one, is that the engine keeps re-deriving facts it already holds. Three separate slices independently proposed the same rect-vs-rect fast path, and the game code that `narrow_phase` was written to replace *already has it* — `paint_tile.cpp:70-78` and `player.cpp:175-184` both say "if the other object is a rectangle, then we have a collision since the AABB check passed". The engine threw that knowledge away on the dominant pair type and pays 64 dot products and 8 square roots for a decision the AABB filter one line earlier already made. Fix the fast path, cache the boxes into a POD array, and stop expanding an OBB to points, and the whole bounding-volume layer stops being the accidental cost centre it is now.

One material caveat throughout: `find_contacts` and `narrow_phase` have no consumer outside `tests/`. The game still runs its own six copied `is_colliding` blocks. Every performance number below is engine-path, and lands when the game migrates — which is the point of an engine-first project, but it means none of this is a measurable frame-time win *today*.

### Fill a POD AABB array once per frame inside find_contacts, instead of two virtual bounding_box() calls per pair  [adapt | value 5 | effort M]
book: §4.2.2 pp.81-82 (cache the transformed BV for the duration of the step; "situations in which objects are being checked for overlap multiple times"); §4.1 p.77 ("use little memory"); §4.2.1 p.80 (the SIMD remark); §4.6.2 pp.117-118 (shared axes turn per-pair projection into per-object projection)
what: When a bounding volume is tested many times in one time step, compute it once, cache it for the duration of the step, and let every query read the cache. Ericson names the cost honestly — caching "nearly doubles the required storage space" — and the condition under which it pays, which is exactly repeated overlap testing. The §4.6.2 k-DOP argument is the same idea generalised: what makes a shared-axis test fast is not tightness but amortisation, n×k projections per frame instead of O(n²)×k. The memory property in §4.1 says the cached item should be nothing but the bound.
where: engine/collision/contacts.cpp:39 calls `a->shape()->AABB_intersects(b->shape())`, which resolves to `Shape::AABB_intersects` (engine/math/matt_math.cpp:122-125): two virtual `bounding_box()` calls returning `RectangleF` by value, then `RectangleF::intersects` → `rectangles_intersect` (matt_math.cpp:156-159) → `test_AABB_AABB` (ericson_math.cpp:21-34). Fix: at the top of `find_contacts`, fill a caller-owned or function-static reused buffer of `struct Aabb { float min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y; }` — one per object — and run the pair test on that array with no virtual call and no `Shape` involved. The signature does not change, which contacts.h:41-45 already promises.
why: `RectangleF` derives from `Shape`, which has a virtual destructor and thirteen virtuals, so every AABB in the engine is 24 bytes on x64 — a third of it a vtable pointer to machinery a broad phase never calls. A cached array of n of them carries n useless pointers, breaks the 4-float stride, and hands the CPU 50% more bytes than the data needs. More importantly, this array IS the input any sweep, grid or tree wants, so it is the precondition for the broad phase that contacts.h names as its one open slot, not a detour around it. It is also the cheap, self-contained slice of the already-filed value-semantics pass on `Shape`: it does not de-virtualise anything, it only refuses to let `Shape` into the broad phase.

T11: a contiguous array of values, reused across frames, zero steady-state allocation, the same trick `std::vector<Contact>&` already uses. T4 pushes back mildly (a second representation of the same fact for one frame), mitigated by the cache never escaping `find_contacts`.
corrections: Slice 6 folded a static/dynamic split into this and claimed it "removes ~34²/2 pointless pair tests". That arithmetic is a rounding error: with 34 statics against 600-1000 live projectiles, skipping the static×static block saves ~578 pair tests out of roughly half a million. ### Guard degenerate edges by edge length, not by testing normalized() against exact zero  [adopt | value 3 | effort S]
book: §4.4.2 p.106 (bias the projected-radius side of the inequality with an epsilon, so near-null axes are disproportionately conservative rather than randomly wrong)
what: When a candidate separating axis comes out near-null, all projections collapse toward zero and the separation inequality becomes 0 > 0. In exact arithmetic that is false; in floating point the accumulated error on each side is random in sign, so a near-null axis can be misread as separating and two overlapping bodies reported disjoint. Ericson's fix biases the radius side of the comparison. The specific 3D source of null axes — the cross product of two near-parallel edges — has no 2D counterpart and is correctly dropped, but the failure mode survives with a different cause: an edge between two nearly coincident vertices.
where: engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:188-192 computes `Vector2F(-edge.y, edge.x).normalized()` and skips the axis if `axis == Vector2F::ZERO`. `Vector2F::normalized()` (engine/math/matt_math.cpp:1363-1371) returns zero only when `length == 0.0f` *exactly*, so an edge of length 1e-7 passes the guard and contributes a unit vector whose direction was computed from a subtraction that lost most of its bits. why: `mattmath::Triangle` has no construction-time validation at all (matt_math.h:770-775 — a bare `Vector2F points[3]` with a defaulted ctor), so a degenerate triangle is constructible today, and a bogus axis either falsely separates the pair or wins the minimum-penetration contest with a meaningless normal. narrow_phase.h already names silently missing collisions as the worse failure — that is the stated reason it throws on circles rather than returning nullopt — so this is the same rule applied to the case it currently misses. Do NOT put an epsilon on the `forwards <= 0.0f || backwards <= 0.0f` comparison: manifold.h's contract and a pinned test fix touching-is-not-overlap at exactly zero, and Ericson's bias is on the radius side of the inequality, not on the boundary of contact.
philosophy: T2 over T8 — a missed collision is the worst outcome the file has already committed to avoiding. Respects T3 by not adding an epsilon where a pinned behavioural contract lives. T4 cost: this would be a third tolerance in a subsystem where `EPSILON` (matt_math.h:47, 1e-4) and `SEGMENT_PARALLEL_EPSILON` (ericson_math.cpp:16, 1e-6) already disagree by two orders of magnitude, so it must be named and its quantity stated.

One thing it did not say: the existing comment at narrow_phase.cpp:186-188 explicitly credits `normalized()`'s zero-in-zero-out contract as "how a degenerate edge is skipped" — so the comment claims a guarantee the code does not provide. ### RectangleRotated: derive the AABB from centre/axes/extents, delete the vector corner cache and the redundant y_axis_  [adapt | value 3 | effort M]
book: §4.2.6 pp.86-87 (Arvo90's centre-radius UpdateAABB, six lines, no corners); §4.2.2 pp.81-82 (caching trades storage for recomputation — never storage *plus* an allocation); §4.4 p.101 (store two of three axes and derive the rest; the OBB is "quite an expensive bounding volume in terms of memory usage")
what: The AABB wrapping an oriented box needs no corners: the centre is unchanged under translation and each new half-extent is a sum of |m[i][j]|·r[j]. ### Triangle::inflate and Quad::inflate are under-inflations; define what Shape::inflate means  [adopt | value 3 | effort S]
book: §4.5 pp.113-114 (a sphere-swept volume is "technically, forming the Minkowski sum of the sphere and the primitive": R = { x : dist(x, inner) <= r })
what: The SSV definition gives a precise meaning to "inflate by r" for any convex shape: the r-offset region, whose faces move out by exactly r along their own normals and whose corners become arcs of radius r. In 2D that is the polygon's Minkowski sum with a disc — a rounded convex polygon. Inflating an AABB by r as a box is the Minkowski sum with a *square* of half-width r, which strictly contains the true offset: conservative, therefore safe as a bound. Moving vertices radially is not the offset at all, in either direction.
where: engine/math/matt_math.cpp:2516-2531 (`Triangle::inflate`) and 2867-2884 (`Quad::inflate`) both normalise the centroid-to-vertex vector and push each vertex outward by `amount` along it. That displaces an edge's supporting line by only `amount * (u·n)` where u is the radial unit and n the edge normal — strictly less than `amount` for any non-equilateral shape, badly less for an obtuse triangle. The result is neither the true offset nor a conservative bound of it. Both bodies also do `edge.normalize()` then multiply by `(point - center).length()`, a round trip that is the identity with worse precision; and `Vector2F::normalize()` (matt_math.cpp:1372-1383) zeroes a zero-length vector, so a vertex sitting on the centroid collapses to the centroid instead of moving out. Fix by offsetting each edge along its own unit normal and intersecting the halfplanes, and say in matt_math.h that `inflate` produces the polygonal outer offset, not the rounded Minkowski sum.
why: This is a correctness hole in a public virtual that nobody currently exercises, so it costs nothing at runtime to fix and would cost a debugging session to hit. The same header line settles what `RectangleF::inflate` means — a conservative box offset — which matters because `RectangleF::inflate` is the one overload with a live caller and is the mechanism behind `Player::bounds()`.

### Reject the point-set bound-fitting family: Ritter, Welzl, hill climbing, and iterate-until-good-enough refinement  [reject | value 1 | effort L]
book: §4.2.5 pp.84-85 (hill climbing over vertex adjacency); §4.3.2 pp.89-91 (MostSeparatedPointsOnAABB, SphereFromDistantPoints, RitterSphere); §4.3.4 pp.98-99 (RitterIterative, NUM_ITER = 8); §4.3.5 pp.99-101 (Welzl); §4.6.4 p.120 (coherence-based k-DOP realignment)
what: Four related families for fitting or realigning a bound over a point cloud: Ritter's two-pass approximate sphere; Welzl's randomised exact minimum sphere with a support set and two recursive calls; hill climbing over a vertex-adjacency structure to track extremal vertices across rotations; and shrink-and-regrow refinement that iterates a fixed count and keeps the best result. All four translate mechanically to 2D — Ritter loses one axis and one squared distance, Welzl's support set drops from 0-4 points to 0-3, hill climbing's adjacency becomes i±1, and the OBB refinement collapses into MinAreaRect, which already finds the global optimum in one pass.
where: Nothing in engine/math or engine/collision should adopt any of them, and this is recorded so a future reader of chapter 4 does not propose incremental extreme-vertex tracking for `RectangleRotated` or for narrow_phase's `Polygon`.
why: Four independent reasons, one per family. Nothing in the engine has a point cloud: `Polygon::MAX_POINTS` is 4, `Quad` and `RectangleRotated` hold four points, `Triangle` three — and for four or fewer points the exact minimum enclosing circle is a closed form, not an iteration. Hill climbing is worse than brute force at three or four vertices, where the exhaustive sweep is three or four comparisons and the whole vertex set fits in a cache line; it also requires pointer-linked adjacency and frame-to-frame walk state, which is precisely the structure T11 says to recast as indices or refuse. Welzl's double recursion has O(n) stack depth (Ericson himself warns about overflow) and mutates its input by random swaps, so it cannot take a `const span`. And RitterIterative is structurally what C2 just deleted: `BRACKET_ITERATIONS = 40` / `ITERATION_POWER = 1.5f` in engine/math/collision_tools.cpp, a fixed-count numerical loop with no convergence proof, tuned by a constant, replaced by the analytic manifold. Ericson's Jacobi carries `MAX_ITERATIONS = 50` in the same chapter. The engine's own history says: when a proposed geometry routine carries an iteration-count constant, look for the closed form first.
philosophy: Rejected under T3, T4 and T11. The one durable residue is Welzl's founding observation, which is free and general: a point already inside a bound cannot change it, so a containment test gates any refit. That is worth remembering if a cached aggregate `bounds()` is ever added — with the T4 warning that a cached bound plus a staleness rule is exactly the hidden-invariant pattern the codebase is shedding.
corrections: Slices 6, 7 and 8 raised these as five separate items across three slices; they are one refusal with four faces and I have merged them. All the individual diagnoses were accurate. Slice 7's related note about `RectangleF::union_of` and `UiContainer::bounds()` being the correct in-tree O(1)-grow precedent is verified and correct — matt_math.cpp:1027-1034 and engine/ui/widget.cpp:96-121, including the empty-child guard whose bug (a parent of one label at (900,400) reporting a box back to the origin) is documented in place.

### Do not rewrite test_AABB_AABB in the min-width form  [reject | value 1 | effort S]

## THEME: narrow-phase  (engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp, resolve.cpp, manifold.h - closest-point, SAT, primitive tests, ray/segment casts, moving-object tests)

The honest headline is that this engine's narrow phase is already the book's answer, and the book mostly confirms it. `engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp` is SAT over both shapes' edge normals with a minimum-overlap manifold and a first-separating-axis early out, and Ericson endorses all three decisions point for point in 5.2.1: test the face normals of both objects, exit on the first separating axis, and keep the least normalised overlap as the contact normal with its overlap as the penetration *estimate*. Better, the slice work establishes something narrow_phase.h does not currently claim: in 2D the edge-normal set is provably exhaustive, because the edge-edge cross-product family that makes the 3D SAT quadratic is empty when nothing is skew. The implementation is complete, not approximate, and the header should say so before a reader who knows the 3D test tries to "fix" it. The book also independently rejects the 40-iteration bisection resolver C2 deleted, naming the parallel-slide worst case - an object moving alongside a surface just outside the termination distance - which is a platformer's steady state.

Two things must be corrected about the state of the code before any of this is costed. First, `engine/collision` has no consumer outside `tests/`. The game carries its own `game/objects/collision_object.h` and six copies of an `is_colliding` block built on mattmath's predicate matrix, and for rect-vs-rect those short-circuit on the AABB test and never reach a narrow phase at all. Every claim in the mined candidates about ~10^6 narrow-phase calls per frame is false; the loop is the engine's designated path and is prospectively hot, not hot today, and there is still no benchmark harness to arbitrate. Second, `mattmath::triangles_intersect`'s notorious four-of-nine edge loop is not a bug: given the containment pass that precedes it, at least two distinct edges of A must participate in any overlap and each crosses two distinct edges of B, so the four tested pairs are exhaustive. It is unlabelled cleverness, not a defect, and filing it as a correctness bug would waste a fix.

What genuinely improves this area falls into three groups. The correctness group is small, cheap, and real: `closest_pt_point_segment` divides unconditionally and returns NaN for a degenerate segment, which reads downstream as "no collision"; `barycentric` divides by a Gram determinant it never checks, and `test_point_triangle` - the one non-rect-rect predicate the game actually runs, on the player-versus-ramp path - inherits that as a silent false negative when the book's own 2D answer is three signed-area sign tests with no division at all; `intersect_moving_AABB_AABB` reports a hit for boxes that can never meet whenever one axis has exactly zero relative velocity, and it is public, uncalled and untested. The performance group has one item that is strictly less work for a bit-identical result - a rectangle contributes two distinct SAT axes, not four, and for an axis-aligned box those two are compile-time literals rather than a per-frame `normalized()` - and one item that is embarrassing rather than slow: the rotated-rectangle path in `polygon_from` routes through `mattmath::Quad`, costing three heap allocations, twelve segment tests and two throw-capable validity passes, inside the function whose own comment says it carries points in an array specifically to avoid heap allocation. The third group is contracts. `ericson_math.h` declares fifteen functions and states a precondition, a degenerate answer or a checked-ness claim for none of them, while `partitioner.h`, `contacts.h` and `narrow_phase.h` next door set exactly that standard and the review already closed a finding demanding it.

The one place the book legitimately pushes back on a declared decision is T3's declined CCD, and it does so from an unexpected direction. The decline is sound; what is not sound is that the speed cap standing in for it was never derived, enforced or tested, and shipped content violates it - a 15px spray blob under gravity 2500 exceeds the 25px combined extent of the 10px platforms in `close_quarters` after 0.6 seconds of fall, so paint falls through thin ledges. The fix that follows is a named constant and a test, not a swept solver, and T3's own rule - a simple model still has exact, documented, tested semantics - is what demands it. The through-line for the whole theme is the same: this narrow phase is right, and almost everything worth doing to it is finishing the sentences it already started - state the axis set is exhaustive, stop testing it twice, stop allocating on one of its four shape types, guard the three divisions in the math beneath it, and write down what each primitive promises.

### Do not cache the last separating axis, and do not cache pairwise distances  [reject | value 1 | effort M]

## THEME: broad-phase  (engine/collision/contacts.cpp find_contacts (the all-pairs loop), partitioner.* - grids, hgrids, quadtrees, k-d trees, sort-and-sweep, BVH, time stamping)

**The honest state.** There is no broad phase, and there is no spatial structure of any kind — I grepped `engine/`, `game/`, `tests/` and `samples/` for grid, quadtree, octree, BVH, sweep, Morton and broad_phase and found nothing but prose. `find_contacts` (`engine/collision/contacts.cpp:15-54`) is a literal `for i / for j = i+1` double loop, and its header says so in its own words and names itself as the insertion point. `Partitioner` splits index ranges for the thread pool and does no spatial work despite the name. One thing every mining slice blurred: the engine collision module has no production caller at all. `game/objects/level.cpp:254-324` runs three hand-written sweeps against a *different*, game-owned `CollisionObject` interface (`game/objects/collision_object.h`, with `is_colliding`/`on_collision`), and `engine/collision/` is reached only from `tests/collision/`. So "the broad phase is missing" is true twice: the structure does not exist, and the function that would host it is not yet what the game runs.

**What the book offers against what this workload actually is.** Chapters 6 and 7 are written mostly for irregular primitive soups in unbounded worlds. This engine is none of that, and the measured facts collapse most of the machinery. All three levels declare `out_of_bounds` as exactly `{-100, -100, 6200, 6200}`. Eighteen, thirty-three and thirty-four collision objects are authored per level and every one of them is static — `Structure::update` is empty and `Structure::is_colliding` returns false unconditionally. Against that, ~600-1000 transient projectiles which, verified in `Projectile::is_matching_collision_object_type`, collide with structures and the opposing player team and never with each other. Collidable extents run from 15x15 up to 100x7000 and 6200x100, which is Ericson's own case 4 — the spread that makes a single uniform grid unsizeable. There are no ray queries anywhere: `test_segment_AABB` and `test_2D_segment_segment` are ported and called only by `matt_math.cpp`'s own predicate matrix. What survives all of that is section 7.5.2 — an array of extents, sorted on one axis, swept forward — precisely because it needs no cell size, no straddling policy, no dedup pass and no build beyond a sort. The tree families (BVH, quadtree, k-d, hgrid) are not rejected for being pointer-linked; they all recast to index arrays and the book asks for that recast itself. They are rejected because at n ≈ 1000 in a fixed square with no ray queries they buy nothing a sorted array does not, and they each bring a tuning decision no benchmark exists to settle.

**The through-line: the cheapest large win is the data, not the algorithm.** Before any pruning, the inner loop makes five virtual calls (`b->for_deletion`, `a->layer`, `a->mask`, `b->layer`, `b->mask`) to reach `layers_collide`, which is a `constexpr` two-instruction test on plain `uint32`s, and four more (`shape()` twice, `bounding_box()` twice through `AABB_intersects`) if the pair survives. In this workload nearly every pair is projectile-against-projectile and dies on the mask, so almost the entire cost is virtual dispatch guarding a bitwise AND. Materialising extent, layer, mask and retirement into one flat per-frame array is a bigger win than the pruning that follows it, changes no contract, needs no ordering decision, and is the precondition for the sweep. Then the sweep. Then, only if measurement asks, the static/dynamic asymmetry — which is real but buys much less once the mask test costs one integer compare.

**Two constraints that must be settled in the same commit as any of it.** Pair-once is currently free from `j = i + 1`, and `tests/collision/contacts_tests.cpp:99-102` asserts contact *position*, not just membership — so a correct, non-duplicating sort-and-sweep still fails that test, and either the assertions become order-independent with `contacts.h` stating that order is unspecified, or the broad phase preserves index order. And containment: sort on the box minimum, break on the maximum, or an enclosing box never finds the small box inside it — which would silently undo the thin-platform case `narrow_phase` was rewritten to handle and that `narrow_phase_tests.cpp:84` pins.

**The blocker, and the thing that beats all of this on value per hour.** `PHILOSOPHY.md:424` already makes benchmarks an obligation equal to tests, and none exists in the tree. Ericson's cost model says exactly what to count — AABB tests and narrow-phase calls — and those are pinnable integers where wall-clock is not; that is a smaller job than the broad phase it would justify and should come first. Separately, the single largest measured win in the whole tree is not in the engine: `StructurePaintable::on_collision` scans up to 1500 paint tiles per projectile contact (levels total 1898 / 4116 / 5230, largest single structure exactly 1500 — I computed these from the level JSON) against a layout `generate_paint_tiles` already lays down as a uniform 1D grid per face. That is the broad-phase idea applied in game code, it needs no engine primitive, and it is hours of work.

### Build the frame's collision records into one flat array before enumerating pairs  [adopt | value 5 | effort M]

The 2D record is four floats. The general rule he states by example: whatever the pair loop needs per object, materialise it once per object, not once per pair.
where: engine/collision/contacts.cpp, `find_contacts`, lines 15-54. The virtual calls it makes per pair come from `CollisionObject::for_deletion/layer/mask/shape` (engine/collision/collision_object.h:32-60) and `mattmath::Shape::AABB_intersects`, which calls virtual `bounding_box()` on both sides (engine/math/matt_math.cpp:122-125).

corrections: Several slices attributed ~10^6 virtual `is_colliding` calls per frame to `find_contacts`. That function has no production caller. The cost is real but it lives in game/objects/level.cpp:254-324, which runs three sweeps against the game's own `CollisionObject` (game/objects/collision_object.h). The engine module is reached only from tests/collision/. Slice 19's proposal to hoist `polygon_from` out of `narrow_phase` is correct mechanically but worth far less than claimed: narrow_phase only runs for pairs that already survived layer/mask and AABB, which is a small number. ### Replace the all-pairs loop with a one-axis sort and sweep over that array  [adapt | value 5 | effort M]
book: 7.5.2, pp.336-337 (SortAndSweepAABBArray); framing at 7.5 opening, p.329
what: Sort the extent array ascending by box minimum along one axis, then for each entry scan forward and stop the instant `min[j] > max[i]`, because no later box can reach back. No cell size, no per-object multi-cell insertion, no straddling problem and no duplicate pairs. The forward scan replaces the textbook active-interval set, so nothing is inserted, erased or allocated during the sweep. Every pair the scan reaches still gets the full AABB test and then the narrow phase.
where: engine/collision/contacts.cpp, replacing the `for (size_t j = i + 1; ...)` at line 25. The three filters at lines 33-49 stay in place and in order; the sweep sits above them.
why: The honest counterweight first: the sweep's stated worst case is objects clustered on more than one axis, and that is this game's steady state - the sprayer has `shoot_interval = 0.0f` (game/objects/weapon_consts.h:44) so it emits one projectile per player per frame from one muzzle, and the mister emits five per 0.1 s. Hundreds of projectiles sit in a tight ball for several frames. But those pairs are all vetoed by the mask, and after item 1 a vetoed pair costs one AND and a compare on data already in the cache line. What the sweep prunes is the pairs that would otherwise reach `bounding_box()` and `narrow_phase`, which are exactly the pairs that cost anything. That is why it is worth it in spite of its worst case, and it is the argument the header comment owes rather than a benchmark it cannot cite yet.
philosophy: T8 headline. T11 - one array reused across frames, allocation-free in the steady state, which is the discipline `contacts` already keeps (contacts.h:32-35). T4 - a sort and a forward scan with one break condition, against a grid that needs a cell size the content cannot supply. Tension with T2 over contact ordering, which item 3 settles.
corrections: Slice 25 rejected the sorted doubly-linked-list variant of 7.5.1 and was right; I verified the reason it gives. `game/objects/level.cpp:340-349` swap-and-pops the whole collision vector every frame, so neither object identity nor array position survives between frames for an incremental structure to exploit, and roughly a hundred projectiles are created and destroyed per player per second.

### Pair-once is structurally free today, and the test pins contact position - not just membership  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 7.7 opening, p.341; 7.1.3, p.290 (time-stamping to avoid re-examining an aliased bucket); 7.1.7, p.299 (test each object as it is inserted)
what: In any partition where one object can occupy several cells, the same pair is enumerated once per shared cell. ### Sort on the minimum and break on the maximum, or containment is silently lost  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]

### Choose the sort axis from centre variance, computed in the same frame  [adapt | value 4 | effort S]
book: 7.5.1 tail, p.338 (variance accumulation during the sweep); 7.5, p.330 and Figure 7.20 (the vertical axis clusters, so do not sort on it)
what: Accumulate per-axis sum and sum-of-squares of AABB centres, form `s2 - s*s/n`, and sort on the axis with the larger value - the axis the objects are most spread along is the one whose sort prunes most. The book computes it during one sweep and applies it on the next call, through a file-scope `gSortAxis`. It also warns that objects resting on a floor cluster tightly on the vertical axis, and in 3D recommends dropping that axis entirely.
where: The extent-array build in engine/collision/contacts.cpp. The axis type already exists: `mattmath::Dimension { x, y }` (engine/math/dimension.h).
why: Two adaptations, both improvements on the source. First, `find_contacts` is a free function over a span with no frame-to-frame memory, and the engine is committed to multi-core work, so the book's mutable global is unavailable - and unnecessary. The extent array is being built anyway, so accumulate the variance in that same pass and sort on the better axis this frame. That deletes the state, the staleness and the global, for one extra pass over four floats per object. Second, the 3D advice ('track x and z, drop y') has no 2D escape hatch: there are only two axes, and gravity guarantees y clusters, because players stand on structures and level geometry is horizontal platforms. corrections: Slice 25 proposed keeping the axis as persistent state next to a `BroadPhase` value type; that is not needed and it would put mutable cross-frame state into a module that is currently entirely stateless. Computing it in-frame is strictly simpler and costs one pass over an array that is already hot.

### Build the benchmark before the broad phase, and count Nv and Np rather than milliseconds  [adopt | value 4 | effort M]
book: 6.1.2, pp.237-238 (T = Nv*Cv + Np*Cp + Nu*Cu + Co); 7.2.1, pp.302-303 ('an appropriate ratio is application specific')
what: Ericson's cost model names what to count: Nv the number of bounding-volume pairs tested, Np the number of primitive pairs, Cv and Cp their unit costs, plus node-update and one-time terms. ## THEME: convexity-geometry  (convexity-based methods (GJK, V-Clip), BSP trees, and geometrical robustness - polygon splitting, triangulation, convex decomposition, welding)

The honest state of this area is that the engine has already taken the single most important lesson this part of the book teaches, and taken it well. `narrow_phase` is SAT over both shapes' edge normals with the minimum-overlap axis kept, running over a fixed four-point `Polygon` on the stack with a first-separating-axis early out, returning a value `Manifold`. Chapter 9 exists to replace exactly the O(n^2) boundary-based method that `mattmath::triangles_intersect`, `quads_intersect` and `rectangles_rotated_intersect` still are, and the replacement is already sitting in `engine/collision/` - it is just not wired to the predicates it supersedes, and not wired to the game at all (`grep -rn engine/collision game/` returns only `partitioner.h`). The 2D completeness result - if a separating line exists between two convex polygons, one parallel to an edge of one of them exists - makes that choice exactly right rather than approximately right, and it is the one thing `narrow_phase.h` does not say.

What the engine has not taken is the precondition that licenses all of it. Convexity is assumed everywhere and checked nowhere: `Quad::is_valid()` tests that the four edges do not cross, which admits a dart, and a dart fed to SAT gets a confident wrong manifold. `Triangle` has no validity check at all. The header contract lists supported shape *types* but never names convexity as the requirement, nor says whether it is enforced - which is precisely the sentence the house style (partitioner.h, contacts.h) demands. Alongside that sits a family of tolerances with no stated relationship: `EPSILON = 0.0001f`, a default parameter that hardcodes the same number instead of naming it, a 1e-6 slab fudge whose own comment concedes the two were never the same quantity, and one relative tolerance in `RectangleRotated::edges_valid`. Verified numerically: one float ulp at the y ~ 5000-7000 the game actually runs at is 4.88e-4, so `EPSILON` is a fifth of a representable step there and adding it changes nothing - while every collision test in the tree runs at coordinates under 800, where it is about 1.6 ulps. Nothing is broken today because `EPSILON` happens only ever to touch normalised or near-zero quantities, but the pinning tests demonstrate nothing about the range that ships. Ericson's discipline - tolerances are a partially ordered set, an absolute epsilon is only valid over a stated coordinate range, and you find out by asserting it - is the cheapest high-value thing in this whole theme.

Most of chapters 8, 9 and 12 does not survive contact with this engine, and it is worth being blunt about why, because the reason is almost never dimension. V-Clip, Dobkin-Kirkpatrick, hill climbing and GJK all die on scale: `MAX_POINTS = 4`, so a four-element linear scan over contiguous floats beats every asymptotic structure, and GJK and Chung-Wang answer separation distance or a boolean when `Manifold` is defined as a normal plus a depth. BSP dies on the shape of the workload: the static set is 18/32/34 axis-aligned boxes plus one right triangle with a world extent already known from JSON, so a general line equation per node buys nothing, and the actual cost is 600-1000 live projectiles that an offline tree cannot hold. Mesh topology and vertex welding die on there being no mesh and nothing to weld - every level coordinate is an exact multiple of 5, and enumerating all static pairs found 16/38/18 exact touches and zero interpenetrations. And the collinear-merge pass that two slices proposed as live work turns out to have no work: the four geometrically mergeable collider pairs in the content all have differing collision types, one of them a jump-through platform between two solid floors, so merging any of them would be a gameplay bug. Ericson's own rule about matching surface attributes is what would have stopped it, and it independently validates the layer/mask/tag design already shipped.

The through-line of what would most improve this area is short. First, build the replay bench - PHILOSOPHY promises benchmarks in words, nothing implements them, and every remaining argument here is about speed and currently unsettleable; the mining agents' own arithmetic about where the cost lies was wrong by three orders of magnitude, which is the argument for the instrument. Second, close the convexity precondition where it belongs, at `Quad`'s constructor and in `narrow_phase.h`'s contract, and add the one missing primitive everything else in this theme sits on - an n-gon signed area (2D Newell collapses to the shoelace sum) - while fixing or deleting `Vector2F::cross`, which returns `(x*o.y, y*o.x)`, is geometrically meaningless, and has zero callers. Third, write down the decisions that close doors: partition objects and never split shapes, store indices and never fragments, canonicalise the pair key before any cell-based enumeration touches the pinned pair-once contract, and decompose a concave level shape at load rather than growing a branch in the hottest routine in the engine. Fourth, clean the three small live defects the book's eye caught - `narrow_phase` materialising a validated `Quad` and two heap allocations for a rotated rectangle in the function whose own comment boasts about avoiding exactly that, `Shape::inflate` being three different operations under one name, and `Player::bounds()` reporting a box 37 times the player's area against a contract that says it is what a broad phase indexes. None of that is a rewrite. It is one primitive, one precondition, four paragraphs of contract, and a bench.

### Order the tolerances, and state the coordinate range each is valid over  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 8.4.3 p.377 (query epsilon must exceed build epsilon); 12.1 p.472 (assert the epsilon is representable); 12.4.1 p.491 (merge tolerance strictly smaller than on-line tolerance)

The query epsilon must be strictly larger than the epsilon geometry was built with, or a primitive placed on one side within build tolerance is missed by a strict query. Separately, an absolute epsilon is only meaningful over a bounded coordinate range, and he gives the cheap check: `assert(v->x - EPS != v->x && v->x + EPS != v->x)`, raising the epsilon until it stops firing. Two asserts in 2D instead of three.
where: `engine/math/matt_math.h:47` (`constexpr float EPSILON = 0.0001f`) and `:62` (the `are_equal(Vector2F, Vector2F, float epsilon = 0.0001f)` default). `engine/math/ericson_math.cpp:16` (`SEGMENT_PARALLEL_EPSILON = 0.000001f`, anonymous namespace, one caller). `engine/math/matt_math.cpp:3619` and `:3631` (`RectangleRotated::edges_valid`, absolute for the dot, relative `EPSILON * std::max(1.0f, length)` for the lengths). `engine/collision/resolve.cpp:18`. `engine/collision/narrow_phase.cpp:140, 189, 212` use no epsilon at all.

### Ericson's hashed uniform grid, in index-array form, as the concrete broad-phase candidate  [adapt | value 3 | effort M]
book: 12.1 pp.469-474 (hashed grid over unbounded cell coordinates; bucket lists as parallel index arrays; cell-size rule and bucket dedup; the coordinate-sum warning)

Collisions are harmless because every candidate is distance-tested anyway, so no perfect hashing and no world bound is needed. Storage is `int first[NUM_BUCKETS]` (head, -1 for empty) plus `int next[MAX_ITEMS]`, insertion being three stores. The honest design is two indices - the ~34 static boxes handled once (or left as a linear list, they are 34), and the 600-1000 live projectiles hashed per frame.
philosophy: T8 (the pair count is the whole problem), T11 (three fixed arrays, no nodes, no allocation after the first frame), T4 (no node-splitting policy the way a quadtree or BVH needs one). ### Multi-contact separation as a 2-variable LP, and Fourier-Motzkin as a test oracle  [archive | value 2 | effort M]
book: 9.4.1.1 pp.394-396 (Fourier-Motzkin elimination); 9.4.1.2 pp.396-398 (Seidel's algorithm)
what: Seidel solves a d-variable LP with m half-space constraints in expected O(d! m): keep the current optimum, and when a new half-space excludes it, recurse on a (d-1)-dimensional LP restricted to that hyperplane. At d=2 the factor is 2, the recursion is exactly one level deep (a 1D interval intersection), and Ericson says outright it is "quite efficient for small d". Fourier-Motzkin eliminates one variable at a time by pairing lower and upper bounds; at d=2 there is one elimination round and no blow-up, and Ericson's own worked example (Figure 9.7) is two 2D triangles.
where: `engine/collision/resolve.{h,cpp}` - `separation` and `separation_along` each answer exactly one contact. The contact list they would consume already exists as `std::vector<Contact>` from `engine/collision/contacts.cpp:10`. The oracle would live in `tests/collision/narrow_phase_tests.cpp`.
why: Two recorded answers, neither to be built now. First: `resolve.h` cannot express simultaneous resolution, so a player wedged in a corner receives two translations that partly undo each other and the order they arrive in decides the result. The 2D LP "find the smallest translation satisfying every contact constraint at once" removes the ordering dependence, is fixed-size and stack-only, and is the known answer the day corner wedging is a reported bug. Second: Fourier-Motzkin gives an independent boolean oracle for `narrow_phase` that shares no code and no reasoning with SAT - convert both polygons to edge half-planes, eliminate x, compare against `has_value()` - and it yields a witness point inside the overlap, which is a stronger assertion than a boolean when a test fails. It is test-only, so its exponential worst case and its allocations never touch a frame.
philosophy: The LP serves T2 (the answer stops depending on iteration order) and T9, but fights T3 and T4 hard - a randomized LP solver is cleverness, and a paint shooter's player may never feel the difference. Do not build speculatively; record so the answer is known when the bug arrives. Ericson's related judgement is worth banking with it: for a pure feasibility question the objective is arbitrary, so the boolean form needs no objective and only the "which translation" form does - at which point the objective is a gameplay opinion (prefer up, prefer smallest), which is exactly the opinion `resolve.h` refuses to hold.
corrections: None.

## THEME: robustness-perf  (numerical robustness (EPSILON and tolerance policy), optimization (cache layout, SIMD, aliasing, branching), collision-system design issues, and GPU-assisted collision)

**The honest state.** The new collision code in `engine/collision/` is better than the book would predict for a first pass. `narrow_phase` is a clean minimum-overlap SAT with a documented containment argument and an early out; `Manifold` and `Contact` are values; `find_contacts` fills a reused vector and allocates nothing in the steady state; `Polygon`'s `Vector2F points[4]` is 13.3.1's "do not store what you can compute" with the reason written in the comment; `Handle<T>` is 13.3.1's "use offsets instead of pointers"; the degenerate-axis guard closes the 2D analogue of Ericson's Figure 10.5; `test_2D_segment_segment` puts every division in the construction and none in the decision, exactly as 11.5.2 prescribes; and the engine collides only fat objects — there is no collidable point or segment type — which is 11.3.4's whole recommendation, arrived at independently. Those are real agreements with the field's standard reference and they should be recorded as such.

What the book exposes is that the *numeric* half of this module was never designed, only inherited. There is no written contract on precision anywhere: `docs/design/PHILOSOPHY.md` promises "documented contracts — edge ordering, winding, zero-length behaviour, what throws" and says nothing about tolerance policy, world-coordinate bounds, or non-finite input. Three unrelated epsilons live in the tree with no shared home, and `mattmath::EPSILON = 1e-4` is smaller than one float ULP anywhere above 1024 while every level runs to 6200. Two range tests are written in the form Ericson names as the NaN trap, so a single NaN coordinate produces an object that is never culled out of bounds and generates a contact against everything in the level. `separation_along` guards its divisor rather than its output, so a dot product one ULP above the epsilon returns a translation ten thousand times the penetration. Four ported routines divide with no guard, and one of them makes `hypotenuse()` throw "Triangle is not a right triangle" on a triangle that is one. None of this is observable today, because every collision test in the tree runs within 100 integral units of the origin and there is not a single non-finite input case anywhere in `tests/`.

Three claims in the tree are simply not true. `PHILOSOPHY.md:71-72` says projectile speeds get capped so nothing tunnels: no cap exists, and the sniper at 2000 px/s over a fixed 1/60 s step covers 33.3 px against a budget of 30 set by the 300x10 platform in every level. `PHILOSOPHY.md:423-425` says benchmarks pin throughput the way tests pin behaviour: there is no benchmark target, no `/GL`, no IPO, and consequently no way to evaluate any performance claim in chapter 13 — which makes chapter 13 unsafe to apply here until there is one. And `manifold.h` states two invariants that nothing checks, because there is not one `assert` call in `engine/` or `game/` and the engine's only failure mode is a throw, which cannot live on the frame path.

**The through-line.** One piece of work orders the rest: `find_contacts` is the sanctioned slot, and the change that belongs in it is 13.5's linearisation followed by 10.4's ordered prune — walk the objects once into a contiguous array of extents plus layer and mask words, then sweep it on x. That single change serves 2.4.1's rule (cut pair count, not per-pair cost), removes every virtual call and pointer chase from the rejection path, needs no cell size chosen for content whose object sizes span 5x5 to 700-wide, and produces exactly the substrate SIMD would later want. It is safe to attempt only because the thing it replaces is already the brute-force oracle 2.7.1 asks for — free right now and gone the moment the loop is edited. Everything else here is either a prerequisite for measuring it (the benchmark, the numeric contract, the world-coordinate sweep), a correctness defect that costs nothing to fix while the module is open (the NaN forms, the unguarded divisions, `separation_along`, three broken dead functions), or a game-side consequence of the engine path not being adopted yet (the six copied `is_colliding` blocks, the twelve `||` chains, the 1500-tile linear scan). Worth stating plainly, because several mining agents assumed otherwise: the game does not use `engine/collision/` at all — `find_contacts` and `narrow_phase` are called only from tests. That makes this work cheaper to land than the candidates thought, and it means the first benchmark run establishes a baseline rather than catching a regression.

### Linearise find_contacts into a flat extent array, then sweep it — the broad phase goes inside, exactly as the header promises  [adapt | value 5 | effort M]

### Keep the all-pairs loop as a reference algorithm, and write 'a filter may only over-report' into contacts.h  [adopt | value 5 | effort S]
book: 2.7.1 pp.20-21 (implement a brute-force reference algorithm and run it in parallel); 10.2 p.419 and 10.4 pp.423-424 (a conservative filter yields a potentially colliding set; false positives cost time, false negatives are wrong answers); 11.3.3 pp.446-447 (a cheap pre-test and an exact test must not disagree at the boundary); 11.6 p.462 first bullet

Honest ranking: it is a wrong answer on a path nothing takes, in a subsystem being retired, so it sits below the items that touch live code.

why: In 2D the 'image' of a scene along a view direction is a 1D line, so chapter 10's convex test collapses into exactly the separating-axis projection the engine computes analytically — sampled where the engine is exact, with a device round-trip where the engine has none, and the chapter concedes its whole family is approximate with both false positives and false negatives. corrections: Slice 31's proposal to move paint coverage into a render target and read back a mip chain at match end is a real technique honestly argued, but it is a large game change plus a new engine capability, it is not on the plan, and the cheaper index-arithmetic fix to on_collision should land and be measured first.
