# The long waits are the benchmark's two clocks — runs 003 and 004, resolved 2026-09-19

**What this is.** The resolution of the waits that
[the reference run](2026-09-19-g6f-reference.md) reported, [the follow-up](2026-09-19-g6f-follow-up.md)
repeated and [the investigation](2026-09-19-g6f-investigation.md) left
unresolved: D3D11's `present` at ~14 ms on every frame, D3D12's and Vulkan's
`begin` at ~15 and ~30 ms, whole-frame p99s of 17, 23 and 32 ms. **They are
not a GPU, driver or synchronisation cost.** They are `LineSweeperFrameBench`'s
own 60 Hz software pacer sitting on top of a synchronised present whose cadence
on that host is exactly 60.000 Hz. Two clocks at one rate make a one-way
ratchet: any stall that fills the present queue moves every later wait out of
the pacer and into the renderer call, where the benchmark counts it as frame
time, and nothing ever moves it back. The same workload costs the same in every
one of the forty repetitions. The evidence is the retained samples of both runs,
unchanged, and four captures on the desktop that reproduce each state on
demand. No instance was launched and no engine code changed.

Read this before either run's tables. Their `record + submit` column measures
the work; their whole-frame column, wherever it reads ~17 ms, measures the
period.

## 1. What the samples establish

Every number below is from `out/cloud/g6f-reference-004/output/results/` and
`…-003/…` as retained; §6 gives the arithmetic.

**The stalled loop runs at exactly 60.000 Hz, not at the display's reported
59 Hz.** In every repetition with a sustained renderer wait, the pacer never
waits (`pacing_wait_ns` median 0) and the renderer call holds the loop at the
pacer's own rate: mean frame-start interval 16.6667 ms in d3d11-001,
16.6679 in d3d12-005, 16.6680 in vulkan-001 (run 004), 16.6669 and 16.6665 in
run 003's d3d11-001 and vulkan-001. The deadline advances one period per frame
unconditionally ([linesweeper_frame_bench.cpp](../../bench/linesweeper_frame_bench.cpp),
`next_frame_ += period`), so start lateness can only hold still if the loop's
rate equals the pacer's — and it holds still: d3d11-001's lateness is
24.104 ms on its first retained frame and 24.079 ms on its last, a drift of
0.4 ppm over a minute. A real scan-out has its own crystal and would drift by
tens of ppm against `steady_clock`.

**Every blocked call returns on one fixed 16.667 ms grid.** Reconstructing
absolute frame times as the cumulative sum of `scheduled_interval_ns`, the
moment `Present` returns in d3d11-002 sits at phase 9.43 ± 0.28 ms on a
16.667 ms grid from sample 1219 to 1519, 9.44 from 1519 to 1819 and 9.40 from
3300 to 3600 — the same phase for the whole locked span, and the span before it
where `Present` blocked with slack (samples 1209–1217) is on the same grid.
d3d11-001 sits within 0.2 ms of phase zero in every 900-frame window of both
runs.

**Everything on that host wakes on millisecond ticks.** In d3d11-001, 3,523 of
3,600 whole-frame times lie within 0.125 ms of exactly 16 or 17 ms, and the
sequence is a strict cycle of two 17 ms frames then one 16 ms frame — 1,193
runs of exactly two long frames, 50 ms per three frames, 60.000 Hz. d3d11-002
keeps the same cycle in every 600-frame window after its onset. The free
repetitions show the same cycle in the pacer's own wake-ups — d3d11-003 has
3,582 of 3,599 intervals on 16 or 17 ms — so the quantisation is the virtual
machine's timer granularity, which every wait on it shares; the desktop,
locked the same way in §3, is smooth, 16.5–16.75 ms with no 16/17 split. That
answers the reference document's question 4: a 17.00 ms median interval
against a 16.667 ms mean is 17, 17, 16, not an overshoot. It is not what
identifies the cadence. The rate is: a cadence equal to the pacer's own rate
within 5 ppm of the guest's clock over a minute, on an instance whose only
display is the vGPU's virtual one, is generated in software. Which component
on `g6f` generates it the samples cannot say; that it is not the L4
rasterising slowly they can, because the same 9,600 particles record and
submit in the same time either way.

**The work is unchanged.** `record + submit` median, D3D11, run 003:
2.14 / 2.08 / 2.19 / 2.15 / 2.11 ms across the five repetitions; run 004:
2.17 / 2.13 / 2.08 / 2.12 / 2.18. The first of each is the locked one. Update
is likewise flat. The entire difference between a 2.4 ms whole frame and a
17.0 ms one is where the wait landed.

## 2. The mechanism

The benchmark paces in software: `pump()` sets `next_frame_` once, `tick()`
waits until it, then advances it one period, and a late frame is never
resynchronised — the loop catches up by not waiting. The engine always presents
synchronised: `Present(1, 0)` on a flip-model swap chain with the default
maximum frame latency of three on both Direct3D backends
([d3d11/device_resources.cpp](../../engine/render/d3d11/device_resources.cpp),
[d3d12/device_resources.cpp](../../engine/render/d3d12/device_resources.cpp)),
`VK_PRESENT_MODE_FIFO_KHR` behind a two-slot frame ring on Vulkan, swap
interval one on GL. So there are two clocks. On the desktop they differ by
parts in ten thousand and the state drifts, slowly, one way or the other (§3).
On `g6f` they are the same rate to better than 5 ppm, and the state is
whatever the history left it.

The history can only go one way. The Direct3D present queue holds up to three
frames ahead of the presentation engine, Vulkan's ring two. It fills when the
loop gets ahead of the engine — a startup stall before the first tick makes
the loop late, and a late loop presents as fast as the engine allows, so a
stall of more than three periods fills it; the engine skipping a vblank fills
it by one, silently. It never drains, because draining needs the loop to
present slower than the engine consumes, and once the loop is behind its
deadlines it never does. Three states follow, with transitions in one
direction:

- **free** — the queue has room, `Present` returns in tens of microseconds,
  the wait lands in the pacer (14–15 ms), lateness stays under a millisecond.
  Sixteen of twenty repetitions in each run.
- **full with slack** — the queue is full, `Present` blocks until the next
  vblank, and the vblank still arrives before the next deadline, so the wait
  is split: d3d11-002 samples 1209–1217 at 7 ms in `Present` and 7 ms in the
  pacer; **gl-003 and gl-004 in run 003, for the whole minute**, their
  `SwapBuffers` returning on the same 60 Hz grid at phase 5.1 / 5.4 / 5.7 ms
  (the reference document's "swap that waits part of a refresh"); gl-004 in run
  004 at 1.44 ms; vulkan-001 in run 004 from its eighth retained frame, a 5 ms
  `begin` wait every sixth frame.
- **locked late** — one more skipped vblank while blocked eats the slack
  (d3d11-002 sample 1218: one `Present` of 24 ms where the nine before it were
  7 ms), the loop is late for good, the pacer never waits again, and every
  whole frame is the period exactly. d3d11-001 and d3d12-005 from their first
  retained frame, d3d11-002 from 1218, vulkan-001 from 1114 in run 004;
  d3d11-001 and vulkan-001 throughout run 003.

Where the wait lands within the renderer is the backend's own shape and says
nothing more. D3D11 blocks in `Present`. D3D12 blocks in `begin_frame`, two
steps removed from the vblank — the fence it waits on is signalled when the GPU
finishes a frame that could not start until the presentation engine released
its back buffer — so its phase carries the GPU's scheduling noise: d3d12-005's
wait toggles between two phases 7 ms apart through pairs of 23.5 and 9.7 ms
frames, 229 such steps, with the loop never leaving the cadence. Vulkan blocks
in `begin_frame` on the frame-slot timeline, whose submission waited for the
acquired image, and the two-slot ring against three FIFO images makes the wait
land on every second frame: run 003's pairs sum to 33.29 ms (p1–p99
32.56–34.17), two periods.

**Why the first process of an API is over-represented.** Four of the six
locked repetitions are a backend's first, and both runs' d3d11-001 are locked
from their first retained frame with a large residual lateness. What a first
process has that later ones do not is the largest startup stall — the driver's
first-use costs, on a host whose caches are cold for that API — and the pacer
carries warm-up lateness straight into the retained minute because nothing
resynchronises it. That is consistent with all four cases and is not directly
observed, because warm-up frames are not retained. It is a sufficient reason,
not a necessary one: d3d11-002 and d3d12-005 lock too, from stalls that the
free repetitions also show — a one-period block in the D3D11 draw path at 438
in d3d11-005 that cost nothing, the same block at 1204 in d3d11-002 five
frames before it locked. The shared quarter-GPU and its compositor stall
occasionally; whether a given stall locks the loop depends only on how much
room the queue had left.

## 3. Local reproduction

Same `x64-release` D3D11 binary and workload as the run, on the desktop the
engine is written on (RTX 5080; display 2560×1440, reported 59 Hz, measured
59.949 Hz — period 16.6808 ms — by the presentation-paced capture). The
pacer's rate against that period decides the state, with the GPU doing
identical work (record + submit median 1.36–1.52 ms in every 300-frame segment
of every capture). Captures under `out/validation/g6f-ratchet/`, one warm-up
second each.

| capture | pacer | what happened | whole frame median | `Present` median | start lateness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `local-d3d11-r60`, 5,400 frames | 60 Hz software, 0.1 % faster than the display | **free** for the first 30 s; **split** from sample 1,774, `Present`'s block growing 4.2 ms per 300 frames as the display's phase drifts 14 µs per frame past the pacer; **locked** from 2,785 | 1.6 ms → 16.68 ms | 0.08 → 15.2 ms | 0.3 ms, then climbing to 36.5 ms |
| `local-d3d11-r59`, 1,800 frames | 59 Hz software, slower than the display | **free** throughout — the queue drains faster than it fills | 1.55 ms | 0.07 ms | under 0.5 ms |
| `local-d3d11-r61`, 1,200 frames | 61 Hz software, faster than the display | **locked** from the first retained frame — the queue filled during warm-up | 16.67 ms | 15.2 ms | +0.287 ms per frame, 333 ms by the end (= 16.681 − 16.393) |
| `local-d3d11-pres`, 1,200 frames | presentation-driven | every frame waits on the display by design | 16.68 ms | 15.2 ms | not measured in this mode |

Whole-frame p99 for this workload on this machine is therefore 1.6 ms or
16.7 ms, decided by which clock is faster and by nothing else. On `g6f` neither
is faster, so there is no drift to make the state visible as a ramp: the phase
freezes wherever a stall left it, which is the constant lateness and fixed
grid of §1. The locked frames here are smooth, so the millisecond quantisation
of §1 belongs to the host.

## 4. What this changes in the earlier documents

The three documents stand as written, with this one added at their head. What
their readings become:

- [Reference §4](2026-09-19-g6f-reference.md), "two repetitions differ in kind,
  not degree": they differ in state. "Whether the presentation engine, DWM or
  the vGPU's display path holds a different mode for a first swap chain" — it
  holds the same cadence for every process; the first process is the one most
  likely to start behind it. [Its §5](2026-09-19-g6f-reference.md) questions 1
  and 2 are answered here; question 3, the GL swap interval, was fixed against
  a premise the samples contradict — gl-003 and gl-004 were already blocking on
  the 60 Hz grid, so the driver's default was already synchronised and the
  request changed nothing measurable; question 4's 0.33 ms is the median of a
  17 / 17 / 16 cycle, because that host wakes every wait on millisecond ticks
  (§1), and there is no overshoot to remove; question 5 stands.
- [Follow-up §2](2026-09-19-g6f-follow-up.md): "the results do not support a
  cause confined to first use of an API" — correct, and the cause is above.
  "GL improves" — gl-004 moved from a 3.5 ms split to a 1.4 ms split, a
  different phase of the same state, not an effect of the swap-interval
  request. "This localizes the CPU duration; it does not identify what delayed
  GPU completion" — nothing delayed it; the fence is behind the compositor.
  [Its §3](2026-09-19-g6f-follow-up.md) pooled table: the frames-over-budget
  column counts locked frames, not slow ones.
- [Investigation](2026-09-19-g6f-investigation.md): its analyzer validation,
  presentation-pacing mode and trace wrapper stand and were used or were
  usable here. Its reading — "the renderer source does not support removing
  the waits" — is right for the wrong reason: the waits are the contract
  working, and the benchmark asked for them by pacing at the cadence's own
  rate. "Splitting submissions is a possible optimization" — irrelevant to
  these stalls. Its remaining experiment, an ETW capture on the EC2 host, is
  no longer needed for this question: the trace would show the wait on the
  flip queue and the compositor, which §1 already establishes from the
  samples. Whether to run one for the *stall* events — the one-period draw-path
  blocks and the compositor's skipped vblanks on a shared quarter-GPU — is a
  separate, smaller question that nothing here depends on.
- The line in `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md` that offered "whole-frame p99 within
  3.2 ms on seventeen of twenty" as the finding and the other three as
  questions: the seventeen measured the work and the three measured the
  period. The p99 of the work itself — update plus record and submit — is
  1.9–3.2 ms in all forty repetitions of both runs, the locked ones included
  (run 003's d3d11-001, 3.23 ms; run 004's d3d12-005, 2.02 ms).

Nothing here reaches the still-unmeasured low tier in PHILOSOPHY; the
`backend-equivalence-2/STATUS.md` box stays open for its own reason.

## 5. What to change, and what not to

- **Nothing under `engine/render/`.** Every backend presents synchronised, as
  the contract says, and every one of them blocked exactly where its shape puts
  the block. The Vulkan submission structure, the D3D12 frame-slot count and
  the D3D11 present path were all suspected and all behave.
- **The benchmark needs one clock.** Software pacing against a synchronised
  present is bistable on any host whose cadence equals the pacer's rate and
  drifts into one state or the other on every host whose cadence does not
  (§3). Two coherent designs: pace in software and present unsynchronised — a
  present-mode choice at device creation that the seam does not offer today,
  `Present(0)`, `IMMEDIATE`/`MAILBOX`, `wglSwapInterval(0)` — so the pacer is
  the only clock and whole-frame measures the work; or pace by presentation,
  which `--pacing presentation` already does, and read `update`,
  `record + submit` and the interval, treating `begin`/`present` as the wait
  they are. Resynchronising the pacer at the end of warm-up is not enough on
  its own: with the queue full it lands the loop in the split state, where
  `Present` blocks by a phase-dependent amount every frame.
- **The analyzer can recognise the locked state outright**: mean interval equal
  to the period, whole-frame median within a few hundred microseconds of it,
  pacing wait zero and lateness constant. It should say "locked to the
  presentation cadence" rather than counting long calls.
- **The reference declaration should not pool a locked repetition with a free
  one**, and until the benchmark has one clock a reference run's whole-frame
  p99 is not a number to quote; its `record + submit` and `update` columns
  are.

## 6. Method and evidence

Absolute times were reconstructed per repetition as the cumulative sum of
`scheduled_interval_ns` from the first retained frame; a call's return time is
that frame's start plus the phases before and including it; its phase is that
time modulo 16,666,667 ns and its millisecond fraction is that time in
milliseconds modulo one. A repetition's state was read from three columns at
once — whether `present_ns` or `begin_ns` exceeds 1 ms, whether
`pacing_wait_ns` (in run 003, the gap between one frame's end and the next
start) is zero afterwards, and whether the whole frame equals the period. The
scripts that produced §1–§3 (`stats.py`, `hiccups.py`, `segments.py`,
`states.py`) and the four local captures are under
`out/validation/g6f-ratchet/`, which is ignored, beside the run evidence under
`out/cloud/`, which is unchanged. The local display's period is the mean
interval of the presentation-paced capture over 1,200 frames.
