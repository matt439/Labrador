# Vader and Ani: character and copy guidance

## What Matthew has told us

Vader is black, caring and protective, and a mummy's boy. Ani is yellow and
younger: jealous, self-centred and often antagonistic toward Vader, while finding
it hard to be apart from him. Vader is often exasperated. They love each other
deeply. These are the owner's descriptions, not traits inferred from coat colour
or names.

One concrete example informs the comedy: when Vader is playing with a toy and
getting attention, Ani takes the toy and then drops it. He wants the attention,
not the toy. Matthew has not supplied Vader's specific response to that incident.
**Do not present an invented reply as Vader's actual real-life reaction.**

Another useful abstract pattern is that Ani demands an equal turn before knowing
what he is volunteering for, even when he does not want the activity itself.
Use that comic structure without the private background anecdote.

## The creative translation

Every staged scene and spoken line is a **fictional proposal**. The compact card
labels and footnotes say so, including for Vader's replies.

- Vader starts with his own interest: showing a sample, doing something well,
  helping someone, or being pleased that Mum might like it. He is not limited to
  weary questions or playing the sensible straight man.
- Ani interrupts to become the centre of attention, then has little prepared.
  His bravado can give way to an appeal to Vader or an insistence on staying near
  him. He should be funny and recognisable, not merely unpleasant.
- Keep the affection present in the ending: an invitation, a place beside Vader,
  or Ani admitting he only wants to lead if Vader is coming.
- Vader may be mildly exasperated in an imagined scene; avoid contempt, cruelty,
  punishment and jokes that imply they dislike each other. Do not make every
  exchange the same jealousy gag.
- Use the Mum reference occasionally, as a source of Vader's own enthusiasm.
  Neither dog needs a costume, a Star Wars role or a collection of dog puns.

## Proposed exchanges and placement

**Dry, Home demo introduction:**

Vader: "I brought a demo."  
Ani: "I'm presenting."  
Vader: "What have you prepared?"  
Ani: "An entrance."

**Playful, Examples preview:**

Vader: "Mum's going to love this."  
Ani: "I'm showing her."  
Vader: "What are you showing?"  
Ani: "Me. You can come too."

**Warm, Home's route into Examples:**

Vader: "I'll show you around."  
Ani: "I'll do it."  
Vader: "After you."  
Ani: "Only if you're coming."

**Alternate interruption, Examples:**

Vader: "Watch this bit."  
Ani: "Watch me."  
Vader: "Show us, then."  
Ani: "...Vader?"

**Alternate equal-turn scene, Examples:**

Vader: "I'm going to practise."  
Ani: "My turn!"  
Vader: "You want to practise?"  
Ani: "No. But I want a turn."  
Vader: "Come on, then. Beside me."

## Placement boundary

The confirmed constraint is complete exclusion of **both characters and dialogue
from technical reference pages**. Home and Examples are candidates. No tone or
production integration is approved. The current comparison now uses only Home
and Examples contexts; the real site's technical pages are untouched.

Put a short exchange after the real sample description and links, so it cannot
interrupt someone finding a command or a contract. No instruction depends on a
bubble. Use static, in-flow scenes with a quiet footer or off option; no automatic
pop-ups, character interruptions over code, or animation. A comic interruption
is a written scene, not an actual interruption of the reader's task.
