The documentation is split by what you are trying to do: build something, understand the
engine's model, finish a task, look up an exact contract, or understand why the engine is the
way it is. Parts of it are written and parts are not, and this page says which.

| Section | When you want to | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Get Started](/docs/get-started/) | Build something that runs, then a project of your own | **Written.** Five steps, each checked on a real machine. |
| Concepts | Understand the model: ownership, objects, states, update and draw, scenes, views, resources | **Not written yet.** The [philosophy](/docs/design/philosophy/) covers the same ideas, written for the engine's developers. |
| Guides | Do a particular job: sprites and text, input, collision, audio, assets, menus, local multiplayer | **Not written yet.** The [samples](/examples/) show sprites, text, input, assets and menus. Neither sample uses collision or audio. |
| [API Reference](/docs/reference/) | Find the exact contract of a type or function | **Written.** Every public header in all ten modules, generated from the source. |
| [Design](/docs/design/) | Understand the reasons behind the engine | **Written.** The three design documents, read from the repository. |
| Troubleshooting | Recover from a problem | **Not written yet.** [Build Labrador](/docs/get-started/build-labrador/#if-something-fails) covers the failures most likely on a first build. |

Every page says at the bottom which revision of Labrador it was built from. Pages that quote the
engine's source, its headers or its design documents read them from that revision, so what they
show is what that commit contains.
