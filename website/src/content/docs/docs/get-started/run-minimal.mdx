The build you just ran also built the minimal sample. Start it from the `Labrador` folder:

```bat
out\build\x64-debug\samples\minimal\MinimalSample.exe
```

A 1280×720 window opens with the line *Hello from the engine.* circling a point, and a hint
along the bottom. Move the point with the left stick of a gamepad, the arrow keys or WASD. Press
**B** or **Escape** and the sample asks whether you really want to quit; **A** or **Enter**
quits, and **B** or **Escape** goes back.

The sample copies its content beside the executable when it builds, so it runs from any folder.

## What you are looking at

The sample is about 450 lines, and most of them are comments explaining the line below. Three
parts are worth knowing before you copy it in the next step.

**`main.cpp` makes the decisions only a game can make**: the window's title and size, where the
content is, and which state runs first. Everything else is the engine's.

<SourceFile path="samples/minimal/main.cpp" from="ApplicationOptions options;" to="return app.run(" />

**A state is a screen or mode of the game.** The engine calls a state's `init()` once, then its
`update()` and `draw()` every frame. `HelloState` puts its two lines of text in a scene and
gives the scene one view covering the whole window:

<SourceFile
	path="samples/minimal/states/hello_state.cpp"
	from="this->scene_ = std::make_unique<Scene>(nullptr, nullptr);"
	to="this->scene_->add_view("
/>

**The question is a second state, pushed on top of the first.** `ConfirmState` knows nothing
about the screen below it. It hands back a `bool` when it closes, and `HelloState` decides what
the answer means. While the question is up, `HelloState` stops updating and dims its greeting,
and because the question does not cover the whole screen, the greeting stays visible underneath.
