In the starter project the window shows a world exactly its own size, and the greeting can be
moved off its edge and out of sight. This page makes the world bigger than the window and adds a
**camera** that follows the greeting, so that the greeting stays in the middle and the world
moves past it. Following is your game's own code, a few lines in `HelloState`; the engine
supplies the camera and draws through it. It takes about 20 minutes.

Before you start, do [Make a first change](/docs/get-started/first-change/), so that you know the
edit, build and run loop and have a copy of your game to go back to.

If you have done the F11 section of
[Colours, the window and the background](/docs/guides/your-window/#full-screen-with-f11), undo it
first, before making a new copy: put `game\states\hello_state.cpp` and
`game\states\confirm_state.cpp` back from the copy you made before that section, as
[Getting a file back](/docs/get-started/first-change/#getting-a-file-back) shows. That section's
`fit_view` and this page's camera both choose the view, and a game has one or the other. Make
again any of that page's other changes to `hello_state.cpp` that you want to keep, such as the
colour, then build, test and run to see that the game works.

Then make a fresh copy with [Make a first change](/docs/get-started/first-change/)'s step 1 line.

Every command runs in the x64 Native Tools Command Prompt, in your project's folder:

```bat
cd /d C:\dev\mygame
```

<Steps>

1. **Put something in the world to see.** With a camera on the greeting, the greeting stays in
   the middle of the window and only what is around it moves, and the starter's world is empty.
   Signposts, each saying where it is, show the world going by. Open the state's code:

   ```bat
   notepad game\states\hello_state.cpp
   ```

   Search with **Ctrl+F** for `this->greeting_ = this->scene_->add(`, in `init()`. Click at the
   start of that line and paste these lines above it, with an empty line after them:

   ```cpp
   	// Signposts every 400 pixels, from -2000 to 2000 across and down, each
   	// saying where it is, so that there is something to see go by.
   	for (int y = -2000; y <= 2000; y += 400)
   	{
   		for (int x = -2000; x <= 2000; x += 400)
   		{
   			this->scene_->add(std::make_unique<Label>(
   				L"+ " + std::to_wstring(x) + L", " + std::to_wstring(y),
   				font_name, Vector2F(static_cast<float>(x), static_cast<float>(y)),
   				resources, labrador::Colour::slate_gray));
   		}
   	}
   ```

   The two `for` lines repeat what is inside them: for each `y` from -2000 to 2000 in steps of
   400, and inside that, for each `x` the same, one label is added at that point, reading
   `+ 400, 800` and so on: 121 signposts over a world 4000 pixels across. Points left of and
   above the window's top-left corner have negative numbers. They are added before the greeting,
   so the greeting is drawn over them.

2. **Keep the hint out of the world.** The hint along the bottom is in the scene too, so once
   the camera moves, it would move away with the world. It is drawn separately instead, over the
   world, without the camera. Still in `init()`, search for `this->hint_ = this->scene_->add(`:

   <SourceFile
   	path="samples/minimal/states/hello_state.cpp"
   	from="this->hint_ = this->scene_->add("
   	to="labrador::Colour::dark_gray));"
   />

   Change the first line to

   ```cpp
   	this->hint_ = std::make_unique<Label>(
   ```

   and the last to end with one `)` fewer:

   ```cpp
   		labrador::Colour::dark_gray);
   ```

   The hint is now made but not added to the scene. Then go to the end of the file, to `draw`:

   <SourceFile
   	path="samples/minimal/states/hello_state.cpp"
   	from="this->scene_->draw(renderer);"
   	to="this->scene_->draw(renderer);"
   />

   Replace that line with:

   ```cpp
   	this->scene_->draw(renderer, [this](int /*view_index*/, DrawList& list)
   	{
   		// Over the world, with no camera: the hint stays at the bottom of
   		// the window wherever the camera is.
   		list.set_camera(Camera::default_camera);
   		this->hint_->draw(list);
   	});
   ```

   What is between `[this]` and the last `}` runs after the scene has drawn the world, with
   `list`, what is being drawn into. `Camera::default_camera` is no camera at all: what is drawn
   after it is placed in the window's own pixels.

3. **Follow the greeting.** Search for `void HelloState::on_suspend()`. The lines just above it
   end `update()`:

   <SourceFile
   	path="samples/minimal/states/hello_state.cpp"
   	from="this->scene_->update(dt);"
   	to="this->scene_->end_tick();"
   />

   Click at the end of `this->scene_->end_tick();`, press **Enter** twice, and paste:

   ```cpp
   	// The camera: the point the greeting circles, in the middle of the
   	// window. A camera's translation is the world point it puts at the
   	// window's top-left corner.
   	const Viewport window =
   		this->app_->viewport_manager()->fullscreen_viewport();
   	this->scene_->clear_views();
   	this->scene_->add_view(window,
   		Camera(this->motion_.centre - window.size() * 0.5f, 1.0f));
   ```

   A **view** is a rectangle of the window and the camera it is seen through. Every frame, these
   lines take away the view the scene had and give it a new one: the whole window, through a
   camera whose top-left corner is half a window up and left of `motion_.centre`, the point the
   greeting circles. That puts the point in the middle of the window. `1.0f` is the camera's
   scale: `2.0f` would show the world twice as big.

4. **Keep the touch markers under the fingers.** A finger is reported in the window's pixels,
   and the markers are now in the world. Search for `marker->set_position(`:

   <SourceFile
   	path="samples/minimal/states/hello_state.cpp"
   	from="marker->set_position(Vector2F("
   	to="touch_marker_offset);"
   />

   Replace those four lines with:

   ```cpp
   			const Scene::View& view = this->scene_->view(0);
   			marker->set_position(
   				view.camera.world_position(points[i].position, view.viewport) +
   				touch_marker_offset);
   ```

   `world_position` turns a point in the window into the point in the world the camera shows
   there. Save and close Notepad.

5. **Change the hint's type in the header.** The state's header says what kind of thing `hint_`
   is: until now, a pointer to a label the scene owns. Open it:

   ```bat
   notepad game\states\hello_state.h
   ```

   Search for `hint_ = nullptr;` and change the line to:

   ```cpp
   	std::unique_ptr<labrador::Label> hint_ = nullptr;
   ```

   A `std::unique_ptr` owns what it points at: the state now owns the hint, since the scene does
   not. Save and close Notepad.

6. **Build, test and run.** Quit the game if it is open, then run the line from
   [Make a first change](/docs/get-started/first-change/)'s step 5:

   ```bat
   cmake --build --preset x64-debug && ctest --preset x64-debug && out\build\x64-debug\game\MyGame.exe
   ```

   It compiles `hello_state.cpp` and `main.cpp`, which includes the header; on a fast desktop the
   line took 5 seconds before the game opened. It ends like this:

   ```text title="Output"
   [3/5] Building CXX object game\CMakeFiles\MyGame.dir\states\hello_state.cpp.obj
   [4/5] Building CXX object game\CMakeFiles\MyGame.dir\main.cpp.obj
   [5/5] Linking CXX executable game\MyGame.exe
   Test project C:/dev/mygame/out/build/x64-debug
       Start 1: MyGameTests
   1/2 Test #1: MyGameTests ......................   Passed    0.01 sec
       Start 2: MyGameSmokeTests
   2/2 Test #2: MyGameSmokeTests .................   Passed    0.78 sec

   100% tests passed, 0 tests failed out of 2
   ```

   The greeting circles in the middle of the window, a little to the right because the label
   starts at the point it circles, with signposts around it: `+ 0, 0` near the top-left, then
   `+ 400, 0` and `+ 800, 0` across the top. Hold the right arrow: the greeting stays in the
   middle and the signposts slide left, past `+ 1200, 400` and `+ 1600, 400`. The hint stays along
   the bottom, and **Escape** still shows the quit question in its usual place.

</Steps>

## Stop at the edge of the world

The signposts end at -2000 and 2000, but the camera goes on following, into empty black. A
camera that stops at the world's edge keeps its corner between two limits. In
`game\states\hello_state.cpp`, search for `#include <cstddef>` and add a line before it:

```cpp
#include <algorithm>
#include <cstddef>
```

`<algorithm>` has `std::clamp`. Then search for `touch_marker_offset =`, near the top, click at the
end of that line, press **Enter** twice, and paste:

```cpp
	// The edges of the world, a little outside the signposts: the camera
	// shows nothing outside them.
	constexpr float world_min = -2100.0f;
	constexpr float world_max = 2300.0f;
```

Last, in the camera lines from step 3, replace the last two lines,

```cpp
	this->scene_->add_view(window,
		Camera(this->motion_.centre - window.size() * 0.5f, 1.0f));
```

with:

```cpp
	Vector2F corner = this->motion_.centre - window.size() * 0.5f;
	corner.x = std::clamp(corner.x, world_min, world_max - window.width);
	corner.y = std::clamp(corner.y, world_min, world_max - window.height);
	this->scene_->add_view(window, Camera(corner, 1.0f));
```

`std::clamp(value, low, high)` gives `value`, or `low` if it is below that, or `high` if it is
above. The corner never goes left of or above the world's edge, and never so far that the window
would show past its right or bottom edge. Build, test and run, and hold the left arrow: once
`+ -2000, 0` reaches the left of the window, the camera stops and the greeting goes on, off the
middle to the left. The greeting itself still walks out of the world and out of sight; keeping
it in is a rule of your game, and [Add a rule and a test](/docs/guides/your-rule/) writes one
that keeps it in the window. The world must be wider and taller than the window, or the two
limits cross; with a world 4400 pixels across, that holds for any window up to 4K.

## Smoother following, and what is next

This camera is locked to the greeting. Games often let the player move a little before the camera
follows, a **dead zone**, or let the camera catch up gradually. Both are more lines of the same
kind in `update()`. The engine has no follow camera of its own: following is a choice each game
makes. A copyable follow camera, with a dead zone and a world edge, in the local-multiplayer
sample is a possible future addition the owner has not decided on (`IDEA#4` in the repository's
<RepoLink path="docs/feature-candidates.md" hash="idea4-a-bounded-dead-zone-camera-follow-component">feature candidates</RepoLink>).

[Two players in your game](/docs/guides/your-two-players/#split-the-screen) gives each player a
camera of their own in half of the window.

## If something fails

<Aside type="caution" title="hello_state.cpp(103): error C2440: '=': cannot convert from 'std::unique_ptr<labrador::Label,std::default_delete<labrador::Label>>' to 'labrador::Label *'">
`hello_state.h` still says `labrador::Label* hint_`: step 5 was not done, or not saved. Change the
line in the header as step 5 shows. After the world's edge, the same message names line 109,
since that section adds six lines above it.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="startup failure: Renderer::set_view_count(7) is outside the capacity of 1 create_device was given.">
The start-up test fails with this, and the game stops with the same message in its error box.
The `this->scene_->clear_views();` line from step 3 is missing, so each frame adds one more view
to the ones before, and the game has room for one. The number in it can differ. Put the line back,
above `this->scene_->add_view(`.
</Aside>

If the hint is missing, or moves away with the signposts, step 2 is not complete: the hint is
still added to the scene, or `draw` does not draw it. If the signposts are missing, step 1's
lines are not in `init()` before the greeting, or are inside another block. The line numbers in
the messages are those of a starter with only [Make a first change](/docs/get-started/first-change/)
and this page's steps 1 to 5 done; other changes move them.

## What was checked

On 31 October 2026, in a starter project made from a starter ZIP of this tree, with Visual
Studio 2026's compiler 19.51 and Emscripten 6.0.12, every step above was made as worded by an
agent editing the files with tools rather than Notepad, and the build and tests ran as shown,
with the project's folder in the quoted output changed to `C:\dev\mygame`. The game was started
and driven with key presses, and screenshots showed the greeting in the middle among the
signposts, the signposts moved after holding the right arrow and then the down arrow, the hint
fixed along the bottom, and the quit question in its usual place after the camera had moved.
After the world-edge section, holding the left arrow stopped the camera with `+ -2000, 0` at the
window's left while the greeting went on to the left and, held longer, out of sight. The
`C2440` and the start-up failure were made on purpose; the number in the start-up failure was 7
in that run. The page was built under `wasm-debug`, its test passed, and headless Chrome showed
the signposts moved after the right arrow was held; the page's run ended through
`Module.onQuit` after **Escape** and **Enter**. Touch was not tried: the markers' new lines were
built, and that they sit under a finger is read from the camera's `world_position`.

A second agent then followed the page as worded in another fresh starter, after
[Make a first change](/docs/get-started/first-change/)'s steps 2 to 4. It built and tested every
step on `x64-debug` and `wasm-debug`, and in headless Chrome the camera followed the greeting and
stopped with `+ -2000, 0` at the window's left. It sent no keys to Windows windows after its
first runs. The page was corrected for what it found: the `C2440` message named line 109, which is
the line only after *Stop at the edge of the world*, and *Before you start* offered to skip the
step that makes the camera follow. The agent that made the corrections repeated steps 1 to 5 in
a third fresh starter, made the `C2440` at line 103, and at line 109 after the world's edge, and
a screenshot of the game's own window, taken without sending it keys, showed the greeting in the
middle among the signposts and the hint along the bottom.
